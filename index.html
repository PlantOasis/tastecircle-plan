1The one idea

We sell results, not reach.

An ad platform charges a restaurant to be seen. We charge them when something actually happens: a student posts a plate standing in their restaurant, a student redeems a deal at the counter, a sorority books a Thursday night. That’s how the apps our size that work already charge (Seated, Upside, Ibotta all bill per verified visit or redemption), and it’s what owners say they want — Toast’s 2025 survey has new customers through deals as the #1 thing restaurants would turn to.

Then we bundle those results into a monthly subscription (Spot Plus $79, Spot Pro $199) so the bill is predictable and we have recurring revenue.

What nobody else has: Beli has rankings but sells nothing to restaurants. Snackpass has points but no social proof. Fetch and Ibotta have brand money but no restaurants and no friends. We have a campus currency earned by posting real meals — which is exactly the proof businesses want to buy.

2Crumbs: the currency underneath everything

Students earn it by eating. Businesses fund it. Students spend it at the places that pay us.

  • Earn: up to 50 Crumbs a day for posting plates and scanning food, plus prizes (500 to the Meal of the Week winner). Every Crumb comes from a real meal.
  • Peg: 100 Crumbs = $1 of funded value. That number is fixed.
  • Fund: restaurants fund rewards (a free side for 300 Crumbs) and bounties; brands fund Quests (“snap your Celsius with lunch: 200 Crumbs”).
  • Spend: at partner spots — so Crumbs pull students toward the restaurants that pay us — or on cosmetics in the shop, which stops Crumbs piling up.

Two stages. Now: restaurants fund their own rewards — a redeemed coffee costs them about 40¢ of goods, and they set a monthly cap so it can never run away. Later: Crumbs paid out by brand Quests and bounties are backed by real cash we hold, and we reimburse restaurants when students spend them. That second stage turns Crumbs into paying customers for restaurants, but it needs a lawyer’s sign-off (stored-value rules). Students can never buy Crumbs that pay for food — that would make Crumbs a prepaid card.

4What we actually keep

8Why students aren’t the customer

TC Plus and the Shop exist, but they’re rounding errors next to one restaurant.

Health and fitness apps convert about 2.9% of users to a paid tier. At $3.99 with Apple’s 15% off, that’s roughly 10¢ per active student per month. One Spot Plus subscription is $79 — the same as about 800 students’ worth of TC Plus. That’s the whole argument for selling to businesses and keeping the app free for students.

9What still has to be decided

Charlie’s questions, in the order they block things.

  • Is the Crumbs-based restaurant model the core? Free Spot → Plus $79 → Pro $199, plus $3 a plate and $1 a redemption. Everything else hangs off this.
  • Proof of visit: a staff code, or a QR that changes every minute? Deals, Bounties and Nights all need one. Today’s code is a fixed string that can be photographed.
  • The $3 split: 150 Crumbs to the student, $1.50 to us — and are bounty Crumbs backed by that cash from day one?
  • Lawyer: for Stage 2 Crumbs (brand-backed, reimbursing restaurants), prize rules for challenges (Apple 5.3), and the CARD Act check on Student Offers (no credit cards marketed on campus).
  • Station or Taste Circle sells reviews, websites and SMS? Recommended: we sell only what’s in the app and refer the rest.
  • Public Spot pages show students’ public plates — fine, with a way for a student to hide theirs?
  • Self-serve checkout on /pricing, or Sales closes every deal? Recommended: both, with a welcome call after every self-serve signup.
  • A separate Stripe account for Taste Circle LLC? Recommended: yes.
