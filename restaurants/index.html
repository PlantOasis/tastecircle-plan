1The pitch in one breath

“Fill your dead hours with OU students, and only pay when they show up.”

Every restaurant near campus has the same two problems: Tuesday at 3pm is empty, and marketing is their second-biggest headache (Toast, 2025). They don’t want another ad platform. They want people in seats they can see, and proof it worked.

So the offer is simple. Students already post photos of your food on Taste Circle. Claim your page for free and answer them. Then, for $79 a month, put a deal on the hours you pick — “free side with any bowl, weekdays 2–5” — and students spend the Crumbs they’ve earned to unlock it. They show a code at the counter. You see every redemption, and once a month you get a report: how many came, when, what they ordered, how you rank against the five spots nearest you.

The line that lands: “You only win if the seat was empty. So we only let the deal run when your seats are empty.”

2Why an empty seat is the product

A restaurant keeps $1–3 of an average $30 check. On a dead-hour check it keeps closer to $20.

Restaurants run on 3–9% net margins: of a $30 check, roughly $10 is food, $10 is labor, $3 is rent, $3 is everything else, and a dollar or two is profit. But labor and rent are paid whether the chair is full or not. A student who walks in at 3pm because of a Crumbs deal only has to cover the food — so that visit is worth $15–20 of profit, not $2. That is the entire economic argument, and it only holds if the visit wouldn’t have happened anyway. Which is why the deal window matters more than the deal.

4Does Plus pay for itself?

A sit-down spot needs about 6 redemptions a month. A café needs about 14.

The value of one redemption is: the share of redeemers who are genuinely new, times the gross profit on their check (about 68% after food cost), minus what the deal cost in ingredients. The share that’s new is the number the window controls. At any hour, deal research puts it around 65% — a third of redeemers were coming anyway. In a dead window it’s closer to 90%, because almost nobody was going to be there.

5How many restaurants the audience can support

The constraint is us, not them. Students redeem a few deals a month, spread across every spot selling them.

If a typical active student redeems about two deals a month, the platform produces 2 × (monthly active students) redemptions in total. Divide that by what one spot needs to break even and you get the number of paying restaurants we can keep happy. Sign more than that and redemptions per spot thin out, the report shows it, and they leave.

7At the counter

Walk-ins Tuesday and Thursday, 2–4pm — the lull is when owners talk. Phone out, their Spot page open.

Open

“Hi — I’m with Taste Circle, the OU food app. Seventeen students have posted photos of your food in the last month; here they are. The page is yours for free if you want it — hours, menu, you can reply to them. Takes two minutes.”

The ask

“The thing most places add is a deal that only runs when you’re slow. You pick the hours — say weekdays two to five — students unlock it with Crumbs they’ve earned by posting, show a code here, you ring it up. Seventy-nine a month, as many redemptions as you get. If six students a month who weren’t coming anyway show up in those hours, it’s paid for. Everything after that is yours; we don’t touch the check.”

Close

“First month’s the trial. At thirty days you get the report — who came, when, what they ordered, how you rank against the five spots around you — and you decide with your own numbers.”

The objections, and the answers

“We already have Instagram.”Instagram shows your food to people who already follow you. These are students who haven’t been in yet, and they only see your deal at the hour you want them. Also — these photos are theirs, not yours; you’re getting content you didn’t have to make.
“Discounts attract people who’d never pay full price.”That’s true for all-day coupons; it’s why Groupon burned people. This one only exists when the seat is empty. A student who comes at 3pm for a free side is a seat you weren’t selling anyway, and half of them bring a friend who pays full price.
“How do I know it’s working?”Every redemption is logged with the time it landed. Your monthly report shows redemptions inside your windows against your normal traffic in those hours. If it isn’t working, you’ll see it before we do — and you cancel.
“What does it cost me per redemption?”Just the food in the deal — a free side is about a dollar fifty of ingredients. We don’t take a percentage and there’s no per-redemption fee on Plus.
“Seventy-nine is a lot.”Square Loyalty is $60–140 with no students attached. Yelp’s upgrade is about $180. One table on one Tuesday covers it.
“Can people cheat the code?”The code changes, and the app checks the student is standing here. A screenshot from last week doesn’t work.

8The pilot

The first ten Plus customers are an experiment to find one number: how many deals a student actually redeems a month.

  • Ten spots, six full-service and four cafés, all with existing plates, within a short walk of campus.
  • First 60 days free in exchange for one thing: they let us publish their results (anonymised) in the next pitch. After that, $79 on a card, cancel any time.
  • Every spot sets at least two windows covering their slowest 10+ hours a week. We help them pick from their own plate timestamps.
  • We measure redemptions per spot per week, redemptions per active student per month (the r in section 5), the share of redeemers who had never posted at that spot before (our proxy for “new”), and whether the restaurant renews at day 60.
  • Success looks like: median spot above its breakeven line by day 45, r ≥ 1.5, and 7 of 10 converting to paid.
Honest caveats to carry into every pitch: the 65% / 90% “truly new” figures come from deal-platform research, not Norman; the $12 / $28 checks are estimates until the first redemption logs exist; and none of these prices has been tested on a buyer yet. The pilot is how we replace every one of those guesses with a measured number.

9What has to exist in the app first

Four things, in this order. Nothing in this document can be sold until the first two are live.

  1. Deal windows. A deal carries days and a time range. Outside the window the deal is hidden in the app and the code will not validate. This is the whole product.
  2. Proof of visit. A rotating code or QR the staff show, plus a location check on the student’s phone. Today’s code is a fixed string that can be photographed and shared. Deals, Bounties and Nights all depend on this.
  3. A redemption log the restaurant can read: time, deal, window it landed in. Also the billing meter for anything pay-as-you-go.
  4. The monthly Spot Report: plates, ratings, rank against the five nearest spots, top dishes, and redemptions inside windows vs. their normal traffic in those hours. The report is the result they are paying for, and it is what makes them renew.

Already in the app: Crumbs, the partner screen with rewards and punch cards, the Crumbs map, plate tagging by location, and the server-side redemption function. The gap is windows, the code, and the report.
